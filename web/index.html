How reet.sh works

Enrol a machine with one command.

Run the agent on a laptop, a VPS or a lab box. It dials out to your controller, so there is no port to open, and reports in with the agents, skills and setups it already has.

Agents, skills and setups stay in sync.

Push a skill to one machine or to all of them, bring back what you find, and review every change before it applies. A new machine ends up with the same reet good toolkit as the rest.

Put your agents to work.

Open a browser terminal to use the agents installed on any enrolled machine, keep sessions running, and pick up where you left off.

The workspace around your agents

Machines, reusable setups, skills and ongoing work belong together. You decide what moves between them.

Your fleet, in view.

Every enrolled machine in one control room: what it has installed, whether it is in sync, and the work already running there.

Setups worth keeping.

Save and share named AI setup profiles through reet. Pull a known setup when preparing another machine, with restore and verification in the CLI.

Skills that travel.

Keep a central skill library, bring machine discoveries back, and choose a source and destinations for distribution.

Sync, on your terms.

Choose one machine or the fleet. Review exact plans and resolve conflicts before applying changes.

Your infrastructure. Your call.

Run reet.sh on your own infrastructure. Keep control of accounts, local shell consent, skills, setup profiles and reviewed fleet changes. One application, one SQLite file, with no telemetry.

Give your machines a home.

Install reet.sh, choose your deployment, then bring your first machine and its AI toolkit into the workspace.
