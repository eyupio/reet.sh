# Reet’s Yorkshire voice

Reet is Yorkshire for “right”: a reet good find is a very good one. The app should feel like a capable Yorkshire neighbour helping you get your tools in order: warm, practical, quietly amused and useful.

The white-rose bookmark, warm colours and bookish typography are part of that identity. The language needs to carry it too, including on busy, populated pages. Do not confine the character to empty states or remove it when polishing layouts.

## Where the character belongs

Use a little local language at visible, everyday moments: the shelf heading, stage descriptions, welcome, empty searches and completed actions. Vary it instead of prefixing every message with “Reet”. One expression per sentence is plenty.

| Moment | Example | Meaning remains clear through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shelf introduction | Ey up. Your skill shelf. | The page’s purpose follows the greeting. |
| Main heading | A reet good toolkit. | The lede explains finding, trying and using skills. |
| In use | Earning their keep. | The stage label stays In use. |
| To test | Give these a proper go. | The stage label stays To test. |
| Empty search | Nowt matches just yet. | Follow with Try another search or clear your filters. |
| Success | Sorted. Note saved. | State the completed action explicitly. |
| Community | Have a nosy. | The eyebrow stays Public community and the lede explains what is public. |

“Ey up”, “reet”, “nowt”, “proper”, “give it a go” and “earning their keep” fit. Use ordinary spelling and natural sentences, rather than a phonetic accent or a string of dialect words. Local character should not become a joke about the user, a novelty mascot or a barrier to understanding.

## Where precision comes first

Keep navigation, buttons and state labels familiar: Account, Admin, In use, To test, Copy prompt, Source, Installed, Missing, Not confirmed, Delete, Restore and Sign out. Do not turn these into puzzles or imply that unknown data is healthy.

Authentication errors, privacy/audience choices, destructive confirmations, backup coverage, scan freshness and install/remove consequences use direct language. A successful settings save is not proof that a backup ran or a machine is in sync. Avoid cheerful reassurance around failures and do not change users’ own names, notes or list content.

The installer and binary follow the same balance: a little warmth around introductions and successful completion, exact commands and clear errors. Existing “Reet, installed” and connection messages belong to this voice; keep shell quoting, machine-readable output and diagnostic detail intact.

## Copy review

Check the everyday signed-in shelf, not just onboarding. Read the heading, lede and stage hints together for repetition. Check phone widths so the personality is visible without adding a tall banner. Preserve the Yorkshire rose and keep the layout restrained. A polished Reet should still sound like Reet.

## Product scope on landing and entry pages

Reet is the self-hosted control room around AI work across a machine fleet:
installed agents, ongoing sessions, reusable AI setups, skills and reviewed sync.
Terminals are a core way to use agents; do not describe the entire product as a
remote-terminal app. Show how a known setup can help bring another machine into
the fleet. Distinguish shipped controls, including observed agent software and a
reviewable plan, from planned verified agent installation, updates, removal,
richer orchestration and fleet-wide agent configuration.

Keep the Yorkshire welcome visible in the main experience: “Ey up” at entry and
a natural “reet good toolkit” or “proper control” in supporting copy. Keep the
white rose, warm colours and bookish type. Familiar labels and concrete outcomes
still come first; do not replace the voice with generic infrastructure marketing
or put dialect into authentication errors and destructive confirmations.
